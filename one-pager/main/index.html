    Calkit: The cure for [reproducibility anxiety]{.highlight} in the age of AI

    Calkit is an open-source research project framework, toolset, and collaboration platform that keeps figures, tables, and values in articles connected to the code and data that produced them, for fast iteration and full transparency.

    By the numbers

    • 37 researchers and groups with projects on calkit.io
    • 5 reproducible publications shipped
    • 7.8 releases per week

    as of 2026-10-24

    The problem

    AI is making scientific output cheaper than ever to produce, shifting the bottleneck to verification and deepening the still-unsolved reproducibility crisis. LLMs will happily generate---and possibly hallucinate---artifacts to back up conclusions without showing all their work (code, data, environments, etc.). It's now essential to make it easy and intuitive to deliver research artifacts with their entire digital paper trail, so evidence for claims can be traced all the way back to its origin.

    Our approach

    Many open science or reproducibility solutions focus on embedding code in documents (literate programming) or helping researchers share and obtain credit for outputs like code and data after a project is done. Calkit takes a more integrated, modular approach. Stages like analysis and writing can remain separate but connected, with information flowing freely between them for fast iteration. Version control (including large data files), environment management, pipelines with hashed I/O, and provenance definition are all included, so researchers don't need to assemble bespoke systems. An optional, self-hostable web app facilitates collaboration during the project and sharing once it's done, like a GitHub for science. With the whole project "single-button reproducible," it becomes much easier to review work done by AI agents or humans.

    A success story

    In the first conference paper I wrote back in 2022, I had a scare where my process of manually screenshotting MATLAB figures and uploading them to Overleaf almost led me to publish inconsistent results when I forgot to rerun some intermediate steps.

    […] Using Calkit significantly relieved my anxiety about inadvertently contributing to the replication crisis; streamlined review, collaboration, and reproduction workflows that would have been otherwise infeasible; and improved the usability and visibility of my work for other researchers. Importantly, Calkit scales to more complex projects, and I can't imagine how I would have kept the 87 figures and 92 numerical results in my dissertation straight without this tool. I also received impressive technical support, including fast and helpful responses to my questions, bug reports, and feature requests.

    ---Rebecca (Becca) McCabe, PhD, Cornell (2026)

    The ask: \$100k to bring Calkit to researchers

    So far we've seen the most interest from researchers who like Overleaf's ease of use but want to connect it to their analyses to eliminate error-prone and tedious manual uploads. In 2027 we'll take Calkit on tour, starting at Caltech and several universities in Boston. In 8 workshops, roughly 200 researchers will connect their analyses to Overleaf with our new browser extension, and we expect to provide follow-up support for about 60 of them. What we learn will be published openly and will drive Calkit's development.
