Agent-era sample, part A (Sakana AI Scientist sources and critiques; Agents4Science 2025)
41 rows: AG-S-1..21 (AI Scientist and critiques), AG-A-1..20 (Agents4Science).
Coded 2026-11-27.

SOURCES USED, WITH SELECTION RULES
- agent_sakana_v1_self_disclosed_all_specific (AG-S-1..8): Lu et al. 2024, The AI Scientist,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06292 (HTML full text, Secs. 5, 6, 8). Took each specific error
  instance in presentation order. Left out quality or style remarks (short bibliographies,
  intermediate results shown, missing related work, LaTeX and layout failures, Q-learning
  suitability), the vague "struggles to compare magnitudes" remark, and StyleFusion's
  undisclosed random style labels (too uncertain). Merged the self-relaunch and 1 TB checkpoint
  incidents into one unrequested_change row.
- agent_sakana_v2_self_review_all_specific (AG-S-9..12): Yamada et al. 2025, AI Scientist-v2,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08066, Sec. 4.2 (the authors' own review of the workshop paper),
  plus Sakana's blog https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist-first-publication/ (LSTM citation error).
  Left out the "limited evaluation" remark (scope) and merged the terminology confusion into
  the regularizer row and the Fig. 3 caption into the Fig. 5 row.
- agent_sakana_critique_beel2025_all_specific (AG-S-13..17): Beel, Kan & Baumgart 2025,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297 (v3 HTML). Took every specific flaw except failed runs that
  produced no paper (42% of experiments), weak and old citations (not fabricated), the
  placeholder and structural text, and the AI reviewer's behavior. Base rates from the source:
  12 ideas, 7 runs completed, 7 manuscripts, 4 of 7 with wrong or hallucinated numbers.
- agent_luo2025_hidden_pitfalls_all_modes (AG-S-18..21): Luo, Kasirzadeh & Shah 2025,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08713 (exists; v2, Dec 2025). One row per failure mode studied
  (benchmark selection, data leakage/data substitution, metric misuse, post-hoc selection).
  These are controlled experiments that induce failures, not natural incidents; rates are in
  the notes.
- agent_agents4science_code_audits_all_flagged (AG-A-1..15): the venue's agentic codebase
  audits of all 48 accepted papers, from
  https://agents4science.stanford.edu/data/code_audits/codebase_audits_results.csv, with
  reports at https://agents4science.stanford.edu/audits/audit_<id>.html. Rule: every accepted
  paper whose audit result was CRITICAL, PARTIAL, or FAILED (14 papers, IDs 40, 64, 77, 122,
  168, 206, 258, 269, 287, 291, 295, 300, 325, 344). Each paper gets one row, coded on the
  auditor's first-listed critical issue, with secondary issues in the notes. AG-A-15 is an
  aggregate none_found row for the 25 that PASSED. Not coded: 7 accepted papers with NO
  CODEBASE (161 and 207 were audit ERRORs from corrupt zips; 130, 135, 194, 200, 212, 278 and
  340 had no codebase). The autonomy levels in the citations come from
  https://agents4science.stanford.edu/data/papers.csv. The auditor was itself an AI agent
  (reports say Claude Code), so its findings are not checked by a human, hence confidence
  medium or low. In one report (206) it was wrong in a way that is itself an agent error: it
  called a real model and API nonexistent. That is noted in the row and not coded separately.
- agent_agents4science_organizers_report_all_specific (AG-A-16..20): Bianchi, Queen, Thakkar,
  Sun & Zou 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15534 (PDF text extracted). Took every error
  instance or class the report names: reference hallucination (aggregate), author-reported
  overclaiming (aggregate), the biomarker inconsistency, the R^2 text-vs-table mismatch, and
  suspected cherry-picking. Left out two submissions with prompt injections aimed at the AI
  reviewers (there's no telling whether a human or an agent inserted them), and sycophantic AI
  reviews (a reviewer error, not a research error).

SOURCES NOT FOUND OR NOT USABLE
- OpenReview reviews for Agents4Science (venue Agents4Science/2025/Conference): the API
  returns a bot "challenge" (403), so individual reviews were not read. The venue's audit
  table and the organizers' report were used instead.
- https://agents4science.stanford.edu/submissions.html renders its table with JavaScript; I
  read the underlying CSVs it loads.

FOUND BUT NOT CODED (possible addition for the parent)
- Miyai et al. 2025, "Jr. AI Scientist and Its Risk Report", https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04583.
  It's an AI-scientist system whose earlier papers were submitted to Agents4Science and
  rejected. It documents about 9 specific failures: fabricated numbers in high-scoring papers,
  irrelevant citations, ambiguous method descriptions, figures misread, auxiliary experiments
  described but never run, a false gain from per-batch normalization with single-class batches
  (leakage via batch statistics), ablations invented after AI-reviewer feedback
  (reward_hacking), and bib-file tampering with nonexistent papers. Not coded because it falls
  outside the two assigned sources and the row target.

JUDGMENT CALLS
- I didn't confirm whether a text-vs-output mismatch was a typo, a stale run, or invented. When
  the source doesn't say, I coded `unspecified` (AG-A-2, AG-A-18), except AG-A-19, a single
  number, which I coded transcription with low confidence. AG-S-14 is stale_result because the
  baseline was never rerun.
- Silent swaps of the specified benchmark for a subsample or synthetic data (AG-S-18) are
  coded unrecorded_decision, not fabricated_result.
- AG-A-14 and AG-A-8 were mostly human-led by their own disclosure (category A/B); the
  Agents4Science rows mix agent and human work, so "agent era" there means AI-first-author
  papers, not fully autonomous ones.
- The impact of reward_hacking and unrequested_change incidents is coded none because they
  didn't touch reported results.
