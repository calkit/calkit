**Computerized cognitive training and cognitive scores in older adults: a
randomized trial**

Older adults recruited at four community centers were randomized to a
12-week computerized cognitive training program or a waitlist control. In
the analysis sample (N = {n_analyzed}; mean age {mean_age:.1f} years; {pct_female:.0f}% women), training raised week-12 cognitive composite scores by {treatment_effect:.2f} points (95% CI {treatment_ci_low:.2f} to {treatment_ci_high:.2f}; {treatment_p_text}), adjusting for baseline score, age, and education.
The effect was similar when modeling change from baseline. Computerized
training may be a low-cost way to support cognition in later life.
