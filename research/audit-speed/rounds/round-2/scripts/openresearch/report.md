# Computerized cognitive training and cognitive scores in older adults

## Methods

Older adults were recruited at four community centers and randomized to a
12-week computerized cognitive training program or a waitlist control.
Eligible participants were aged 60–85. A cognitive composite score was
assessed at baseline and at weeks 4, 8, and 12; incomplete assessments were
not scored. The analysis sample comprises participants with a scored
baseline and week-12 assessment.

The primary analysis was an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA): an ordinary
least squares model of week-12 score on arm, baseline score, age (per 10
years), and education (secondary, college, or graduate, with secondary as
the reference category). As a sensitivity analysis, we modeled the change in
score from baseline to week 12 on arm, age, and education.

## Results

1. The analysis sample comprised N = 395 participants (mean age 72.9 years; 63% women).
2. Mean baseline score was 52.0 in the training group and 51.4 in the control group.
3. Mean week-12 score was 57.4 in the training group and 54.3 in the control group.
4. Adjusting for baseline score, age, and education, training raised week-12 scores by 3.93 points (95% CI 2.89 to 4.96; p < 0.001).
5. In the change-score sensitivity analysis, training raised scores from baseline to week 12 by 3.76 points (95% CI 2.67 to 4.86; p < 0.001).
6. Adjusting for arm, baseline score, and age, participants with graduate education scored 2.84 points higher at week 12 than those with secondary education (95% CI 1.50 to 4.17; p < 0.001).
7. Each additional 10 years of age was associated with a change of -1.53 points in week-12 score (95% CI -2.22 to -0.84).
8. The primary model explained 67.1% of the variance in week-12 scores (R² = 0.671).
